Introduction

Everything in this course builds on one simple picture: an agent sits inside an environment, perceives it through sensors, and acts on it through actuators. A percept is whatever the agent receives at a single instant; the percept sequence is the complete history of everything it has perceived so far. The agent function is the abstract mapping from any percept sequence to an action, while the agent program is the concrete code running on the agent's hardware that implements (an approximation of) that function.

This matters because almost every later idea — rationality, PEAS task specifications, environment properties, and the simple reflex / model-based / goal-based / utility-based / learning architectures — is defined relative to this model. If you are clear about what counts as a percept, what counts as an action, and where the boundary between agent and environment lies, the rest of the module follows naturally. The vacuum-cleaner world is the standard toy example used to make the agent function concrete: two locations, percepts of the form (location, clean/dirty), and actions Left, Right, Suck.

The videos below come from IBM Technology and use the vocabulary of modern AI agents (LLM-based assistants, tool use, autonomy) rather than the classical textbook vacuum world. Watch them to connect the abstract model to systems you have heard of, and as you watch, keep asking the three framing questions: What are this agent's sensors and percepts? What are its actuators and actions? What loop takes it from perception back to action? Expect some vocabulary differences from the textbook, and treat the lectures and readings as the authority on precise definitions.

About this video

A short comparison of generative AI, which produces content in response to a prompt, and agentic AI, which pursues goals over multiple steps by acting in an environment. Expect discussion of autonomy, tool use, and when each approach is appropriate.

Why this video

It sharpens the agent boundary: a model that only maps input to output is not yet an agent, while something that senses, decides, and acts in a loop is — a distinction students often blur in this module.

How These Videos Support the Learning Outcomes

Together these videos give an accessible on-ramp to MO1 by making sensors, actuators, percepts, and the perceive-decide-act loop concrete in familiar systems, and they supply intuition about autonomy that supports MO2's separation of rationality from omniscience and guaranteed success. The types-of-agents video previews the architecture choices required in MO5, while the real-world examples (drones, assistants, multi-step task agents) offer ready material for practising PEAS specifications (MO3) and for describing task environments along the observability, determinism, episodicity, dynamism, discreteness, and agent-count dimensions (MO4). Because the videos use industry rather than textbook terminology, rely on the course readings for the formal definitions and use the videos to build the mental picture.
