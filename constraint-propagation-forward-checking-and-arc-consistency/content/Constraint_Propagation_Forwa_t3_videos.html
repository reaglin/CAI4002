Introduction

Arc consistency is the workhorse form of constraint propagation in CSP solving. An arc (X, Y) is consistent when every value left in X's domain has at least one compatible partner value in Y's domain; values with no partner can be deleted immediately because they cannot appear in any solution. The REVISE subroutine performs exactly this deletion for a single arc, and AC-3 repeats REVISE over a queue of arcs, re-adding neighbouring arcs whenever a domain shrinks, until the queue empties or some domain becomes empty.

This matters because arc consistency sits one level above forward checking in pruning power. Forward checking only filters the domains of variables directly adjacent to the one just assigned; AC-3 pushes those deletions onward through the constraint graph, often detecting failure much earlier. The trade-off is cost: AC-3 runs in roughly O(ed^3) time for e binary constraints and domain size d, so you pay more per search node in exchange for a smaller search tree. Beyond arc consistency lie path consistency and the general k-consistency hierarchy, which prune more but cost more still.

The videos below come from Stanford's CS221 CSP sequence. As you watch, keep three questions in mind: what exactly gets deleted and why, which arcs must be re-examined after a deletion, and what the three possible outcomes of propagation are (empty domain and failure, all singleton domains and a solution read off directly, or reduced domains that still need search). Also note how the lectures connect propagation to variable and value ordering heuristics, since in practice these are used together.

About this video

This is the core lecture for the topic: it introduces arc consistency as a local consistency condition on binary constraints and shows how enforcing it removes values that cannot participate in any solution. Expect a worked treatment of the propagation process, including how deleting a value from one domain forces re-examination of neighbouring arcs, and how propagation can terminate in failure, in a solved problem, or in a reduced but still ambiguous problem. The discussion is framed in terms of the factor-graph style of CSP used throughout CS221.

Why this video

It is the one video in the set that directly targets arc consistency itself, covering the defining condition, the deletion (REVISE) step, the ripple effect that motivates AC-3's queue of arcs, and the possible termination outcomes.
