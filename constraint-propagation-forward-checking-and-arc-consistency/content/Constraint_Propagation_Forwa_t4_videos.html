Introduction

So far in this module you have treated propagation and search as separate ideas: backtracking search assigns variables one at a time, while forward checking and AC-3 prune domains. This topic puts them together. Maintaining arc consistency (MAC) runs arc consistency after every assignment, so the search tree shrinks as inference removes values that can no longer participate in any solution. Layered on top are ordering heuristics — minimum remaining values (MRV) and the degree heuristic for choosing which variable to assign next, and least constraining value (LCV) for choosing which value to try first.

The other half of the story is intelligent backtracking. Plain chronological backtracking undoes the most recent decision even when the real culprit lies much further up the tree; backjumping and conflict-directed strategies use the recorded conflict set to jump back to a decision that actually matters. All of these techniques cost something per node, so the practical question is always whether the inference you pay for buys more search savings than it costs.

As you watch, keep a running comparison in mind: for each heuristic, ask what information it uses, when it is computed (statically once, or dynamically at each node), and what it would cost on a large CSP. Try to sketch how you would assemble these pieces — propagation level, variable order, value order, backtracking policy — into a single configurable solver you could benchmark.

How These Videos Support the Learning Outcomes

Together these videos extend the propagation ideas of MO1 into full search: the Stanford overview establishes the CSP formalism that local consistency operates within, the dynamic ordering segment explains how MRV, degree and LCV choose branches as domains change, and the 4-Queens walkthrough lets you trace forward checking and heuristic choices on a concrete instance in the manner required by MO2. The explicit discussion of trade-offs in the short video, plus the per-node cost framing in the Stanford material, supports the comparison of forward checking and MAC in MO4 and gives you the reasoning you will need to interpret nodes-expanded and runtime benchmarks for MO5. Note that AC-3's queue and REVISE subroutine (MO3) are covered in the module readings rather than in these videos; use the videos to understand where that algorithm is invoked during search.
