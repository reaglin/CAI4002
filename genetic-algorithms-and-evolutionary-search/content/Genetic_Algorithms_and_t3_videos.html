Introduction

A genetic algorithm has no understanding of the problem you are solving — all it knows is the number your fitness function returns. That single number decides which individuals survive, which get to reproduce, and therefore where the search goes next. Designing it well is the difference between a GA that converges on a genuinely good solution and one that wanders, stalls, or cheerfully optimises the wrong thing.

This topic works through what a fitness function must do: give a graded signal (not just pass/fail) so the algorithm can tell a near-miss from a disaster, shape a landscape that rewards partial progress, and control selective pressure through scaling so the population neither converges prematurely nor drifts aimlessly. It also covers the practical complications you meet in real problems — infeasible solutions handled by penalty terms or repair operators, several competing objectives that must be weighted or traded off, fitness values corrupted by noise or sampling, and the need to validate and tune your evaluation before trusting its results.

The videos below come from a channel focused on metaheuristic optimisation, where fitness evaluation plays exactly the same role it does in GAs. While watching, keep asking: what exactly is being measured, how is a candidate solution turned into a number, and how would that number change if the solution improved slightly? Those questions are the core of fitness function design, and they transfer directly to the GAs in this module.
