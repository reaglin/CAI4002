Introduction

Once a knowledge base is written in propositional logic, the central question becomes: does what we already know force some new sentence to be true? That is the entailment question, and this topic covers the main algorithmic answers to it. Model checking attacks entailment semantically, by enumerating truth assignments (the truth table) or by searching more cleverly, as in DPLL-style backtracking with unit propagation and pure-literal reasoning. Resolution attacks it syntactically, by converting sentences to conjunctive normal form and repeatedly applying a single inference rule until a contradiction appears.

The two views are connected by one key idea: KB entails alpha exactly when KB together with the negation of alpha is unsatisfiable. That is why satisfiability solving and proof by refutation are really the same job seen from two directions, and why SAT solvers built on DPLL are the engine behind so much practical automated reasoning. The topic also covers the restricted but very useful case of Horn and definite clauses, where modus ponens drives forward and backward chaining in linear time without any need for full resolution.

As you watch, keep track of three things: what form the sentences must be in before an algorithm can run (CNF for resolution, definite clauses for chaining), whether the algorithm is reasoning about models or manipulating symbols, and what the cost is in the worst case. Try to re-derive each small example by hand before the video finishes it.
