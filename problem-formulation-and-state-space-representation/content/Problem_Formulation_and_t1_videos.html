Introduction

Before an AI agent can search for a solution, someone has to describe the task in a form a search algorithm can actually work with. Problem formulation is that translation step: taking a messy real-world goal — get a delivery van across a city, tidy a room, arrange pieces on a board — and expressing it as a state space, an initial state, a set of actions, a transition model, a goal test, and a path cost. The same underlying task can be formulated in many ways, and the choice you make largely decides whether the resulting search finishes in milliseconds or never finishes at all.

That is why this topic sits at the front of the module. The subtopics walk from the real task to the formal problem, through the standard components, and then to the harder judgement calls: what detail to abstract away, what assumptions the classical formulation quietly makes (a fully observable, deterministic, discrete, static world with a single agent), and how to evaluate a formulation before you commit to running a search on it. Tractability is the thread running through all of it — a state space that grows combinatorially can make a perfectly correct formulation useless in practice.

The video selected here comes at tractability from the complexity-theory side. While you watch, keep asking how the ideas map back to formulation choices: when a problem is described as "hard," what exactly is growing, and what would it mean for your own state-space design if the number of reachable states grows exponentially with the size of the input? Treat the video as background on why formulation matters, not as a substitute for the five standard components, which you will practice applying directly in the rest of the module.

About this video

This Quanta Magazine explainer introduces computational complexity through the P versus NP question: what makes a problem easy to solve versus merely easy to check, and why some problems appear to require effort that grows explosively with input size. Based on its title and description, it covers how computer scientists measure the difficulty of problems and where the practical limits of computation lie. Expect conceptual illustrations and examples rather than formal proofs.

Why this video

It gives students an accessible, well-produced grounding in why problem difficulty scales the way it does, which is exactly the intuition behind the "Formulation Determines Tractability" subtopic.
