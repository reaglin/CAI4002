Introduction

Earlier in this module you traced AI from its philosophical roots through the optimism of the 1950s and 60s and the funding collapses known as the AI winters. This topic picks up the story where handcrafted rule-based and expert systems ran out of road, and explains how a different strategy — letting systems learn patterns from data — came to dominate the field. You will see how machine learning, then deep learning with multi-layer neural networks, and most recently large-scale foundation models each represented a shift in how AI capability is produced.

A central idea here is that no single invention caused the modern boom. Three enablers had to arrive together: enormous digitized datasets, cheap parallel compute (notably GPUs), and algorithmic advances such as backpropagation at scale and the transformer architecture. Landmark results — ImageNet in 2012, AlphaGo in 2016, and the generative AI systems released from 2022 onward — are best read as evidence of those enablers compounding rather than as isolated breakthroughs.

As you watch, keep a vocabulary list. These terms are used loosely in public conversation but precisely in this course: AI, machine learning, deep learning, neural network, foundation model, large language model, generative AI, pretraining, and fine-tuning. Note how each video nests these concepts inside one another, and ask yourself which current systems are narrow tools and which are claimed to be more general — a question you will need again later in the module.
