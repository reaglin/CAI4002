Introduction

Artificial intelligence is no longer confined to research labs — it recommends what you watch, screens medical images, flags suspicious transactions, steers vehicles, tutors students, and drafts text and images. This topic surveys those application domains so you can see where AI delivers genuine value, where it merely automates something mundane, and where claims outrun the technology. Looking across sectors also makes the pattern clear: most deployed systems are narrow, trained for one task on data from one setting, and they degrade when conditions change.

In this course, the survey matters for two reasons. First, it grounds the historical and definitional material from earlier in the module — the systems you encounter in healthcare, finance, transportation, education, and creative work are concrete test cases for deciding what counts as "intelligent" and whether a system is weak or strong AI. Second, every domain carries its own risk profile: biased training data in hiring and lending, opaque reasoning in clinical decisions, privacy exposure in student and patient records, and unclear accountability when an autonomous vehicle errs.

While watching, keep a running list of three things for each example: what task the system actually performs, what data it needs, and what fails or who is harmed when it gets things wrong. Notice also the cross-cutting strengths — speed, scale, pattern detection in high-dimensional data — and the cross-cutting limitations — brittleness, data dependence, lack of common sense, and the need for human oversight.

How These Videos Support the Learning Outcomes

These videos supply the concrete, contemporary examples the module's outcomes depend on. The IBM use-case survey and the TED talk on embodied AI give you a stock of real deployed systems to classify as narrow or general intelligence under the module's capability criteria (MO3), and they let you test the thinking/acting and human-performance/rationality distinctions against actual products rather than abstractions (MO1, MO2).
