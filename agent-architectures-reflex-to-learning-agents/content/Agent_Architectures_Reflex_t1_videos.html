Introduction

An agent is anything that perceives its environment through sensors and acts on that environment through actuators. That simple definition carries a lot of machinery with it: a percept is a single snapshot of sensor input, a percept sequence is the complete history of everything the agent has perceived, and the agent function is the (usually abstract) mapping from percept sequences to actions. The agent program is the concrete code that implements that function, and it runs on top of an agent architecture — the physical or computational platform of sensors, actuators, and compute. Keeping these four ideas distinct is the single most useful habit you can build in this module.

Rationality in AI is not the same as omniscience or perfection. A rational agent selects the action expected to maximize its performance measure, given the percept sequence it has seen so far and whatever built-in knowledge it has. To reason about that precisely, we use PEAS — Performance measure, Environment, Actuators, Sensors — to pin down the task before we design anything. Environment properties (fully vs. partially observable, deterministic vs. stochastic, episodic vs. sequential, static vs. dynamic, discrete vs. continuous, single- vs. multi-agent) then constrain which architectures are even viable: a partially observable world, for example, forces the agent to maintain internal state.

The videos below approach these ideas from different angles. The IBM videos are practitioner-facing and use modern LLM-based agents as their running examples, so as you watch, translate their language back into the textbook vocabulary: ask yourself what counts as a percept, what the actuators are, and what performance measure is implied. The DeepCast video is a short, course-style treatment of the classic agent taxonomy and is the closest match to the formal framing used in this course. Where the videos differ in emphasis or terminology, treat that as useful practice in mapping industry vocabulary onto precise definitions.

About this video

An introductory overview of what AI agents are and how they differ from ordinary programs or standalone models, presented by IBM's Maya Murad. Based on its title and framing, it is likely to cover the perceive-reason-act loop, the role of tools and actuators, autonomy, and how an agent decides what to do next in pursuit of a goal. Expect concrete, contemporary examples rather than formal mathematical notation.

Why this video

It is the most widely viewed accessible introduction to the agent concept and gives you an intuitive grip on the sense-think-act cycle before you formalize it as percepts, percept sequences, and the agent function. IBM Technology is a reliable producer of conceptually clear explainer content, and the video's emphasis on tools and environment interaction maps directly onto the actuators and sensors entries of a PEAS specification.

This video walks through five categories of AI agents and the kinds of autonomous functions each supports, using real-world application scenarios such as a package-delivery drone. The delivery-robot style example is also one of the task environments you will be asked to specify in PEAS terms.

How These Videos Support the Learning Outcomes

Together these videos build the vocabulary and intuition targeted by MO1, giving you multiple framings of percepts, actions, agent programs, and the architectures that host them. The IBM overview and the DeepCast lesson supply the agent-type distinctions needed for MO4 and preview the learning-agent idea developed in MO2, while the applied drone and delivery scenarios give you realistic task environments to decompose into Performance measure, Environment, Actuators, and Sensors for MO3. Because the videos repeatedly tie agent capability back to what the environment demands — observability, uncertainty, and change over time — they also set up the trade-off reasoning you will need to justify an architecture choice against rejected alternatives in MO5. Note that none of the videos gives a formal PEAS table or a four-component learning-agent diagram; use the assigned readings for those details and treat the videos as orientation and reinforcement.
