Introduction

Once you can name the agent architectures — simple reflex, model-based reflex, goal-based, utility-based, and learning — the harder question becomes: which one should you actually build? This topic gives you a decision framework. You start from the environment profile (observable or partial, deterministic or stochastic, episodic or sequential, static or dynamic, discrete or continuous, single- or multi-agent), translate the task requirements into capabilities the agent must have, and then select the simplest architecture that meets those requirements.

The framework matters because the "most sophisticated" architecture is rarely the right answer. A learning agent needs training data, a feedback signal, and ongoing maintenance; a utility-based agent needs a defensible utility function; a model-based agent needs an internal state model somebody has to keep correct. Every added capability buys performance at the cost of complexity, data dependence, and long-term upkeep. A good justification names the alternatives you rejected and states, in terms of the environment profile, why they were rejected.

The videos below come from IBM Technology and are industry-facing rather than textbook-facing. Watch them with two questions in mind. First, when a speaker describes an agent type or system design, ask what environment properties are quietly being assumed. Second, notice where added autonomy or added machinery is presented as a trade-off rather than a free upgrade — that framing is exactly what you will reproduce when you defend an architecture choice in a worked scenario.

A walkthrough of five agent types — commonly presented as simple reflex, model-based reflex, goal-based, utility-based, and learning agents — illustrated through practical examples such as drone delivery. Why this video

Video 2: Generative vs Agentic AI: Shaping the Future of AI Collaboration

IBM Technology · 7:19 · 1,461,675 views · 1y ago · captions — Watch on YouTube

About this video

Contrasts generative AI systems, which respond to prompts and produce content, with agentic systems that pursue goals, take actions, and operate with greater autonomy. The discussion is likely to cover where each approach fits and what collaboration between them looks like in practice.

Why this video

The generative-versus-agentic contrast is a modern restatement of the same question you face in this topic — how much autonomy and goal-directedness does the task actually require? — which supports the subtopics on weighing performance against complexity and justifying a choice over rejected alternatives.

Video 3: AI Model vs Agentic Harness: What Actually Drives AI

IBM Technology · 8:30 · 176,575 views · 1mo ago · captions — Watch on YouTube

About this video

Martin Keen separates the underlying AI model from the surrounding "agentic harness" — the tooling, memory, control loop, and scaffolding that turn a model into a working agent. The video argues that system behavior is driven as much by this surrounding architecture as by the model itself.

Why this video

It makes the engineering cost of an architecture visible, which is exactly what the data, feedback, and maintainability subtopic asks you to weigh; it also reinforces that "agent architecture" means the whole program structure, not just a learned component.
