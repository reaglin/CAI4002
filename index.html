CAI4002 - Intro to AI

Outcomes:📋 Course and module learning outcomes

Modules

Open a module to see its topics and everything in it.

  • Module 1

    History and Foundations of Artificial Intelligence

    Traces the historical development of AI from its origins through modern breakthroughs, and examines definitions of intelligence, the Turing test, and the distinction between strong and weak AI. Surveys current trends, application domains, and the ethical considerations shaping AI practice today.

    45 items
  • Module 2

    Python Programming Toolkit for AI Development

    Reviews the Python constructs, data structures, and libraries (scikit-learn, AIMA Python) used throughout the course for implementing AI algorithms. Covers setting up the development environment, IDEs, and version control workflow for assignments and projects.

    25 items
  • Module 3

    Intelligent Agents, Rationality, and PEAS

    Introduces the agent-environment model, defines rational behavior and performance measures, and applies the PEAS framework to specify agent tasks. Classifies task environments as observable, deterministic, episodic, static, discrete, and single- or multi-agent.

    19 items
  • Module 4

    Agent Architectures: Reflex to Learning Agents

    Compares simple reflex, model-based reflex, goal-based, utility-based, and learning agent architectures and the problems each is suited to solve. Students design agent programs for sample environments and justify architecture selection.

    25 items
  • Module 5

    Problem Formulation and State Space Representation

    Teaches how to cast real problems as search problems using states, initial state, actions, transition model, goal test, and path cost. Introduces the search tree, node expansion, and the criteria of completeness, optimality, time, and space complexity.

    22 items
  • Module 6

    Uninformed Search: BFS, DFS, and Uniform Cost

    Examines breadth-first, depth-first, and uniform-cost search algorithms with their data structures, traces, and complexity analysis. Includes hands-on implementation of each strategy on classic problems such as the 8-puzzle and route finding.

    19 items
  • Module 7

    Depth-Limited, Iterative Deepening, and Bidirectional Search

    Covers depth-limited search, iterative deepening as a memory-efficient complete strategy, and bidirectional search for reducing branching cost. Compares all uninformed strategies and provides guidance for selecting among them.

    16 items
  • Module 8

    Heuristic Functions, Best-First Search, and A*

    Introduces heuristic evaluation functions, admissibility, and consistency, then develops greedy best-first search and the A* algorithm with proofs of optimality. Students design and compare heuristics for puzzle and pathfinding problems.

    16 items
  • Module 9

    Local Search: Hill Climbing and Simulated Annealing

    Explores optimization-style search over complete states, including hill climbing variants, local maxima and plateau problems, and simulated annealing with its cooling schedule. Applies local search to large-scale problems where path is irrelevant.

    19 items
  • Module 10

    Genetic Algorithms and Evolutionary Search

    Presents population-based search using encoding, fitness functions, selection, crossover, and mutation operators. Includes case studies applying genetic algorithms to scheduling, design, and optimization problems.

    22 items
  • Module 11

    Constraint Satisfaction Problems and Backtracking Search

    Formulates problems as variables, domains, and constraints, covering unary, binary, and global constraint types with examples such as map coloring, Sudoku, and scheduling. Develops backtracking search with variable and value ordering heuristics.

    22 items
  • Module 12

    Constraint Propagation: Forward Checking and Arc Consistency

    Covers inference techniques that prune domains during search, including forward checking and the AC-3 arc consistency algorithm. Examines exploiting problem structure and local search for CSPs through an implementation project.

    22 items
  • Module 13

    Logical Agents: Propositional and First-Order Logic

    Introduces knowledge bases, propositional syntax and semantics, entailment, and inference by resolution and model checking. Extends to first-order logic with quantifiers, predicates, unification, and forward and backward chaining.

    23 items
  • Module 14

    Knowledge Representation Structures and Expert Systems

    Surveys ontologies, semantic networks, frames, scripts, and production rule systems for organizing knowledge. Examines expert system architecture, inference engines, rule-based reasoning, and approaches to handling uncertainty.

    22 items
  • Module 15

    Probabilistic Reasoning with Bayesian Networks and HMMs

    Reviews probability fundamentals, conditional independence, and Bayes' rule, then builds and performs inference on Bayesian networks and Hidden Markov Models. Applies decision theory and utility theory to making rational decisions under uncertainty.

    25 items
